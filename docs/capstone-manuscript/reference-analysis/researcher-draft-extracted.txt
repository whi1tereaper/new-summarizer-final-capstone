P0018 [Normal] RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
P0020 [List Paragraph] This chapter presents the analysis and discussion of the results related to the development of the NLP-Based Article Summarization Tool for NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus. It discusses the difficulties that users may encounter when processing lengthy academic materials and explains how the proposed system was planned to address these concerns. The chapter also presents the development of the system through the Agile Development Life Cycle, including the planning and design decisions, the initial features developed, and the issues encountered during the current stage of implementation. Through these discussions, the chapter provides a clear view of how the identified problem guided the researchers from the initial concept toward the development of the proposed solution.
P0022 [List Paragraph] Problems Encountered in the Existing System
P0024 [Normal] The current process of reading and summarizing lengthy academic materials at NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus can be challenging for students, particularly when dealing with research articles, journals, and other academic readings that contain a large amount of information. Students often need to read the entire material, identify important ideas, highlight significant details, take notes, and rewrite the information in their own words before they can produce a summary. This manual process can require considerable time and effort, especially when several academic readings are assigned within a limited period.
P0026 [Normal] Students may also experience difficulty determining which information should be prioritized from a lengthy material. Academic articles often contain main ideas, supporting details, explanations, examples, and technical terms that may make it difficult to immediately recognize the most relevant information. As a result, students may spend additional time reviewing the same material to understand its central idea and determine which details should be included in their summary.
P0028 [Normal] Another concern is the possibility of leaving out important information or misunderstanding the original meaning while creating a summary. Since students manually decide which parts of the material to retain, shorten, or remove, the final summary may not always represent the source content accurately. This can become more challenging when students need to process multiple readings at the same time, as they have to manage both the amount of information and the time needed to summarize each material.
P0030 [Normal] Although existing summarization tools are available, they may not always match the specific needs of students working with academic materials. Some tools may provide limited options for controlling the length or format of the generated summary, while other helpful functions may not be available in a single platform. These limitations can make it difficult for students to use one tool for different reading and reviewing needs.
P0032 [Normal] These concerns highlight the need for an article summarization tool that is more focused on the academic reading needs of the intended users. The proposed NLP-Based Article Summarization Tool for NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus is designed to assist students in processing lengthy academic materials by helping them identify important information and view it in a more manageable form. Through its planned summarization and supporting features, the system aims to make academic content easier to process, review, and understand without requiring students to manually summarize every lengthy material from the beginning.
P0034 [Normal] 2. The Design and Development of the NLP-Based Article Summarization Tool for NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus through the use of Agile Development Life Cycle.
P0036 [Normal] The researchers applied the different phases of the Agile Development Life Cycle in the development of the NLP-Based Article Summarization Tool for NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus. The Agile approach was used to organize the development into manageable stages, allowing the researchers to work on specific parts of the system, review the results, identify issues, and make necessary improvements as the development progressed. Each stage of the Agile Development Life Cycle is defined and explained as follows:
P0039 [Normal] 2.1 Planning Stage
P0041 [Normal] The researchers began the planning stage by identifying the difficulties students may encounter when reading and summarizing lengthy academic materials. Academic articles, journals, and other educational readings often contain large amounts of information that require students to carefully read, identify important ideas, take notes, and create their own summaries. The researchers considered these difficulties in determining the need for a tool that could assist users in processing lengthy academic content in a more manageable and organized manner.
P0043 [Normal] Furthermore, the researchers established the objectives, scope, and limitations of the proposed system based on the identified needs. The team identified the main functions to be incorporated into the system, including article summarization, adjustable summary length, output format selection, the Nutshell feature, Filipino translation, Text-to-Speech, summary history, and user feedback. These functions were planned to support users in reviewing academic materials while providing different ways to access and understand the generated information.
P0045 [Normal] The researchers also discussed how the system would be organized and how the different features would work together. Particular attention was given to the Nutshell feature, which was planned to provide a concise and simplified version of submitted content to help users understand its main idea more easily. The researchers also considered the arrangement of the system pages, the presentation of summarized results, and the information that would be included in the system's analytics.
P0047 [Normal] As a result of the planning stage, the researchers established a development direction for the proposed system and identified the features and components to be developed in succeeding stages. The development activities were then arranged according to the Agile Development Life Cycle, allowing the researchers to work on the system gradually while reviewing the results and addressing issues encountered during implementation.

P0067 [Normal] The Use Case Diagram of the NLP-Based Article Summarization Tool for NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus illustrates the interaction between the system and its two main access roles: the User and the Admin. The User represents the primary users of the system, including students, teachers, and researchers who may use the tool to assist them in processing lengthy academic articles. Through the system, users can log in or register an account, submit article text, URLs, or supported documents, generate summaries, adjust the summary length, select an output style, use the Nutshell feature, translate the generated summary into Filipino, and access audio playback. Registered users can also view their summary history and provide a 1-to-5-star feedback rating based on their experience with the generated results. Guest users may use the summarization function without an account, while account registration allows users to access functions that require saved records.
P0069 [Normal] The Admin, on the other hand, has a separate dashboard designed for system management and monitoring. Through the Admin Dashboard, the administrator can manage user accounts, view system analytics, monitor summary records, and view the feedback ratings submitted by users. This allows the administrator to observe how the system is being used and identify areas that may require further improvement. Importantly, the Admin is not restricted to administrative functions alone; the administrator can also access the main summarization features of the system as a regular user when needed.
P0071 [Normal] This use case structure establishes a clear separation between user-level and administrative functions while maintaining shared access to the core summarization services. It also connects the system’s operational functions with its monitoring and feedback mechanisms, allowing user-generated feedback and system activity to become part of the basis for continuous improvement. In this way, the proposed system is designed not only to generate summaries, but also to provide an organized workflow for article processing, result management, user interaction, and system monitoring within a single platform.

P0088 [Normal] Figure 7 illustrates the Level 1 Data Flow Diagram of the NLP-Based Article Summarization Tool for NEUST Peñaranda Off-Campus, showing how data moves through the system’s main processes. User Management handles registration and login data, account information, and account credentials, allowing users and administrators to access the system according to their roles. Article Input receives article text, URLs, and supported document files such as PDF and DOCX, which are then passed to Article Summarization for processing. The summarization process applies the system’s NLP functions and summarization settings to generate the summarized article.
P0090 [Normal] The generated summary is then passed to Summary Management, where summary data and summary history are handled for user access. Users may also submit a 1-to-5-star feedback rating, which is processed through Feedback Management and forwarded to the Analytics process. The Analytics process organizes relevant summary and feedback information and provides analytics results, summary records, and feedback ratings that can be accessed by the Admin. This structured flow allows the system to manage user accounts, article inputs, NLP-based summarization, summary records, user feedback, and system analytics in an organized manner.

P0098 [Normal] Figure 9. Level 2 – Login Management
P0100 [Normal] Level 2 of Login Management involves both the User and Admin entering their credentials to access the system. If a user forgets their password, they can enter their registered email address to request a One-Time Password (OTP) for password reset. The user then enters the OTP and creates a new password, which is stored in the Users table. This process allows users and administrators to securely access their accounts while providing a password recovery function for users who forget their credentials.
